Assistant Professor of Economics and Finance

Anna Pestova

I am an applied economist at Toulouse Business School (TBS Education), with research interests in household finance, housing markets, and wealth inequality. I study how changes in housing wealth shape education. I am interested in the evolution of local housing wealth inequality and the causes and consequences of reduced access to homeownership among young people. Previously, I studied how financial and geoeconomic shocks affect sanctioned economies and the firms and households within them.

I received my PhD in Economics from CERGE-EI in 2024.

Research

Working papers & revisions

Housing Bust and Long-Term Human Capital Scarring in the U.S.

With Alexander Popov · 2026

Paper PDF
Abstract

Falling house prices during the 2008–2011 housing bust reduced college attendance among first-year college-age children of homeowners relative to children of renters. In areas with the steepest declines, the education gap persisted for at least a decade. The affected cohort also had lower earnings and was less likely to work in education-intensive sectors.

Macroeconomic and Distributional Impact of Sanctions News: Evidence from Russia

With Mikhail Mamonov · 2026

Paper PDF Slides PDF Earlier version
Abstract

We use high-frequency trading data on Russian sovereign bonds around U.S. sanctions announcements to identify sanctions news shocks. Russia’s sovereign spread rises before announcements, suggesting information leakage. We estimate declines of 2.5% in output, 3% in consumption, and 5% in investment, with the largest effects among the most productive firms and the wealthiest households.

Potato to the Rescue: Home Production and Child Nutrition During Deep Economic Crises

With Mikhail Mamonov and Ella Sargsyan · 2026

Revise and resubmit at the Journal of Development Economics

Paper PDF
Abstract

Using individual-level data from post-Soviet countries in the 1990s, we find that households growing potatoes on suitable soil were able to buffer the effects of the crisis on child health. The effect is specific to potatoes and to cohorts exposed during the crisis. We find that households consumed the potatoes they grew.
